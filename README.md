# kitchen-table

Play paper Magic: The Gathering over webcam. Point a camera at your playmat, share the table code, and click any card on screen to read it.

Everything is one file, `index.html`. There is no build step and no server of your own: video goes directly between players (WebRTC), and PeerJS's free cloud service introduces players to each other.

## Running it

Use **Chrome or Edge** on a computer. Captions, voice commands, and the AI card matcher depend on features those browsers have.

### Hosted (recommended)

1. On GitHub, open this repo's **Settings > Pages**.
2. Under **Build and deployment**, set **Source** to **GitHub Actions**.
3. Every push to `main` runs **Deploy site**, which publishes the page in about a minute. To publish without a change, open **Actions > Deploy site > Run workflow**.
4. The page is live at `https://<your-username>.github.io/kitchen-table/`.
5. Open it, click **Sit down**, then **Copy invite link** and send it to your friends.

**The all-cards picture index** (so clicks can find any card, not just commanders and cards someone looked up) is only built when you ask: open **Actions > Build card index > Run workflow**. The first build covers every Magic card and takes a few hours; it saves progress as it goes, so you can stop it and run it again later to carry on. After that, a run only adds cards the index doesn't have yet, so picking up a new set takes minutes. Deploy site keeps whatever index was built last.

Hosting matters. The phone camera QR code and the all-cards index only work from the hosted page, and Chrome remembers the microphone permission there. Opened as a local file, captions may ask for the mic every time they restart.

### Local file

Double-click `index.html`. Players each open their own copy and type the same table code.

## Features

- **Digital table (no real cards)**: the first time you open the site, the screen splits into two big buttons: **Digital Magic** (`play.html`) on the left, over a digital table in play, and **WEBCAM Magic** (real cards) on the right, over four webcam views of playmats (both scenes use real card pictures from Scryfall). It shows every time you open the site (an invite link goes straight to its table), and both lobbies have a **Back to the main screen** link. When you sit down at either table your mic starts muted and your camera hidden; the **Mic** and **Camera** buttons turn them on (captions start once your mic is on). On the digital table you pick an official deck or paste a decklist (Moxfield, Archidekt, MTG Arena, or any "1 Sol Ring" list; a "Commander" heading or `*CMDR*` marks the commander, and a Sideboard section is kept apart), cards are looked up on Scryfall, and decks can be saved in your browser. Sitting down at a Commander table asks **Who is your commander?** (prefilled from the list, with its legendary cards to pick from, or type any card; two for partners). A commander that's in the decklist comes out of the library and starts in the command zone. You can change it from the command zone until the game starts. It's a manual table like Cockatrice or Untap.gg: players follow the rules themselves and the table handles the cards, with voice chat and no webcams or voice commands.
  - **Zones**: library, hand, battlefield, graveyard, exile, command zone. Drag cards between them, click to tap, double-click to play from hand, double-click your library to draw (a single click only says how, so a stray click never draws), right-click (or press and hold) for counters, transform, face down, token copies, the tokens a card makes, reveal, and moving cards anywhere. Keys: D draws, U untaps all, T taps the card you're pointing at.
  - **Arrows**: right-click your card, **Target with an arrow**, then click any card or player; everyone sees the arrow in your color. Arrows clear when the turn passes (or with **Remove its arrows**).
  - **Attaching**: **Attach to...** on an Equipment or Aura, then click the creature (yours or another player's). It peeks out behind the creature (up and to the right, one step further for each attachment) with its name on the part that shows, and moves with it; point at it to bring it forward and see it large. Attached to another player's creature, a dashed line links them. Dragging it away or **Unattach** frees it.
  - **Other players' cards**: right-click another player's card to tap or untap it, add or remove counters, destroy it, exile it, return it to its owner's hand or library, or **Gain control of it**. A card you control but don't own has a ring in its owner's color, and when it leaves the battlefield it goes to its owner's graveyard, hand, exile or library. **Give control to** hands your own card to another player, and **Give back** returns a borrowed one. Every action shows in the game log.
  - **Library tools**: draw, shuffle, scry, surveil, mill, look at the top cards (take any of them to your hand, the battlefield, the graveyard or exile, optionally revealed, and put the rest on the bottom or top in a random order or the order shown), search (then shuffle), reveal or exile the top card.
  - **Game flow**: Start game picks a random first player, shuffles and deals 7, and offers London mulligans (the first is free in multiplayer Commander). Next turn passes the turn; a phase tracker shows where the active player is.
  - **Helpers** (Help to turn off): untap and draw at the start of your turn (no draw on the first turn of a two-player game), a lands-played counter that warns on a second land, a mana pool that tapping a land adds to, commander tax on the commander in the command zone ("Tax +2" after one cast), commander damage per opposing commander (also changes your life), poison, and a game log of every move.
  - **Casting**: double-click a card in hand (or right-click, **Play** or **Cast it**): permanents go onto the battlefield, and instants and sorceries go to your graveyard. A spell with "target" asks you to click its target (a card, a player's area, or your own life for yourself; empty space for none), an Aura asks what to enchant and attaches to it, and the target flashes on every screen with a sound for its player.
  - **Combat**: **Attack** on your turn, then click your attacking creatures (they tap unless they have vigilance) or **Attack with all**, then **Combat damage** asks the players you're attacking. Only the defending player decides the damage, on their combat screen (also **Take or block** on the combat bar): each attacker with its picture, power/toughness and keywords, a **Block with** picker, and what will happen as they block: who dies and what gets through, worked out with flying and reach (who can block), menace, "can't be blocked", protection from colors, trample, deathtouch, first and double strike, lifelink, indestructible, infect (poison) and wither (-1/-1 counters), and several blockers. One button applies it all: their life and commander damage, both sides' dead creatures to their graveyards, and lifelink. **Take no damage** covers fogs, and **Call off attack** ends an attack. Other abilities are worked out by hand.
  - **Official decks**: the lobby's **Official decks** tab lists every official preconstructed Commander deck and every official 60-card deck (starter kits, challenger decks, planeswalker and theme decks, intro packs, duel and event decks, Game Night, Clash Pack, Planechase and Archenemy decks, World Championship decks, Brawl decks, and digital-only MTGO and Arena decks), newest first, each with a description (colors, commander, main themes, main creature type, lands and average mana value, and whether it's legal in Standard). Search by deck, commander, card or theme, filter by Commander, Standard-legal or all 60-card decks, by color, or to good first decks, see a deck's cards, and **Use this deck** fills in the list, name and format. The list (`precons.json`) is built from [MTGJSON](https://mtgjson.com)'s deck files and Scryfall's card data by `tools/build-precons.mjs` every time the site is published; if that fails, the last one published is kept. Your own lists are on the **My decks** tab.
  - **Effects on other players**: cards that do something to other players ("each opponent draws a card, then you draw a card for each opponent who drew", like Cut a Deal; "each player discards", "each opponent loses 2 life", "each player sacrifices a creature", mill, gain life, and "target player"/"target opponent" versions, which ask who) get it in their right-click menu, read from their rules text, and an instant or sorcery with one offers it when you cast it. **Group effects** does any of these by hand. Each player's own browser does what happens to them: draws, mills and life right away, and discards (or at random) and sacrifices ask them to choose.
  - **Terms**: every Magic keyword and game term with what it means (the same list as the webcam table's Terms tab, in `terms.js`, plus Scryfall's keyword lists), searchable, and viewing a card lists the terms on it.
  - **Big cards and pop-outs**: point at any card (on the table, in your hand, or in "Keep this hand?") and it pops out large next to it (Help to turn off).
  - **Power/toughness**: creatures show what they are now, counting +1/+1 and -1/-1 counters and changes (green when bigger than printed, red when smaller). Right-click, **Power/toughness...** for pump spells (until end of turn, gone when the turn ends) or lasting changes; other players can change yours from their screen.
  - **House rules**: picked before the first game and kept with the table: friendly mulligans (a 0, 1, 6 or 7 land hand goes back for free), a free first mulligan in every game, the first player drawing on turn 1, no take-backs (turns off Undo), starting life, the commander damage and poison that knock a player out, and table agreements (proxies, no infinite combos, no extra turns, no mass land destruction, no stax, no fast mana before turn 3, no tutors) shown to everyone.
  - **Deck check**: the lobby warns (never blocks) when a Commander deck isn't 100 cards, repeats a card, has cards outside the commander's colors or banned cards, or when a 60-card deck is under 60, has more than 4 copies or a sideboard over 15, or has cards banned, not legal or restricted in a format you pick (Standard, Pioneer, Modern, Legacy, Vintage, Pauper). It uses Scryfall's legality data.
  - **Sideboarding**: a list's Sideboard section is kept apart; in 60-card games, **Sideboard** swaps cards for the next game, and **Sideboard first** in "Keep this hand?" deals a new hand from the new deck.
  - **Monarch, initiative, day and night**: **Monarch...** tracks them for everyone. Combat damage to the monarch or the player with the initiative moves it to the attacker, the monarch draws when their turn ends, and the initiative reminds its holder to venture each upkeep.
  - **Turn alert**: when your turn starts and the tab is in the background, a chime, a browser notification (if you allow them) and a marked tab title.
  - **Sounds** (Help to turn off): your turn, being attacked, being targeted, and chat.
  - **Maximum hand size**: ending your turn with more than 7 cards asks you to discard down to 7 (or keep them, for a Reliquary Tower; a house rule turns it off).
  - **Out of the game**: at 0 life, at the poison limit, or at the commander damage limit from one commander (house rules can change those), you're asked whether you're out ("Not yet" for a Platinum Angel), and **Concede** is always there. Players who are out are marked Out, skipped in turn order (a turn in progress passes on), the monarch and initiative pass on, and the table says who's the last one standing. When a player is out their cards leave the game: their own cards leave the table (including ones others took control of), and cards they borrowed go back to their owners. **Back in the game** undoes it (their own cards come back).
  - **Undo**: the Undo button (or Ctrl+Z) takes back your last moves (up to 30). What other players did to your cards and cards handed to another player can't be undone.
  - **Chat and watching**: text chat under the log for anyone who can't talk, and **Just watch** in the lobby joins a table without a seat or a deck (watchers see everything players show the table, and can chat).
  - **Game records**: **New game** asks who won; the result (players, decks, commanders, length, turns, first player) is saved in every player's browser. **Stats** shows wins and win rates per player, commander and deck, average game length, how often the first player wins, and exports CSV. **Back up (file)** and **Restore from a file** move records to another device, and when you sit down with other players, each sends their records of games you played together.
  - **Phones and tablets**: one column, smaller cards, other players scroll sideways, and the log, chat and card view open from the **Log** button. Press and hold a card for its menu (including **View card**).
  - **Coming back**: refresh or rejoin with the same table code and your game continues where it was.
- **Layout**: the cameras take the middle of the screen. The top bar has the game controls in the middle (game clock, **Pass turn**, which lights up when it's yours to pass, and **Dice**, which includes Roll for first) and your own controls on the right (mic, camera, captions, and **More** for your phone camera, flipping your camera, a new game, and leaving). The side panel has tabs: **Card**, **Log** (table log and damage history), **Captions**, and **Table** (game mode, monarch, turn clock, game records). A tab you're not looking at shows how many new things arrived there. **Hide panel** gives the cameras the whole width; looking a card up brings the panel back.
- **Commanders**: set yours from your camera ("Set commander"). Under its name everyone sees its color identity as mana symbols and its base power/toughness (or a planeswalker's starting loyalty); hover for the card, click to read it.
- **Commander damage**: under your life total there's a small **-** and **+** for each other player. **+** counts 1 commander damage from that player and takes 1 off your life; **-** takes one back. Quick clicks go in the damage history as one entry, 21 or more turns red, and everyone sees it on your camera. Not shown in 60-card games.
- **Game modes**: Commander (up to 4, 40 life), **1v1 Commander** (2 players, 30 life), Brawl (30 life), and 60-card formats (20 life). The host (the lowest occupied seat) picks the mode in the Table panel, and it syncs to everyone. Switching mid-game starts a new game for everyone. A third player trying to join a 1v1 table is sent back to the lobby with a message.
- **Phone as your camera**: pick **My phone** in the lobby's **Camera** list (it's picked for you when no webcam is found), or use **More > Use my phone as the camera** at the table. Scan the code with your phone and tap Start camera; the phone only sends video, and your computer keeps your mic, captions, and controls. If a player stops getting your phone's picture, their screen asks the phone to reconnect to them.
- **Connections repair themselves**: if the video link between two players drops mid-game (a network hiccup), it reconnects by itself, and a camera that freezes or gets paused starts playing again.
- **Full screen**: the ⛶ button next to a player's name makes their camera fill the screen (the page goes full screen, the side panel stays so clicking a card still shows it). Press ✕ or Esc to go back to everyone. Only on your screen.
- **Movable cameras**: drag the ⠿ handle on a player's camera onto another camera to swap them. Arrow keys work on the handle too. Your layout is only on your screen and is remembered for that table.
- **Table log**: life gains and losses are logged for every player, grouped into one line per burst of clicks, for example `Rick lost 3 life (40 → 37).` Turns are numbered per player and for the whole game, for example `Rick's turn 3 (game turn 7).`, and the active player's camera shows "Your turn 3". A new game starts the count over.
- **Leave and come back**: rejoin the same table code during a game and your life, counters, commander damage, and commanders come back, and the turn stays where it was. Your browser saves your part of the game, and the other players remember you too, so it works after a refresh, after leaving, or from a different device. When a player drops on their turn it stays their turn; anyone can pass it if they don't return. A new game starts fresh.
- **Counters**: poison, experience, energy, plus **Other counters**: +1/+1, -1/-1, loyalty, defense, charge, time, lore, oil, shield, stun, finality, level, quest, storage, age, fade, doom, rad, ticket, or any name you type. Counters can go on you or on a named card, and everyone sees them.
- **Card recognition by picture**: click a card's art on any camera, or drag a box around a card (or just its art) for a tight spot. Like SpellTable, a ring pulses where you clicked, then snaps to an outline around the card it found and a scan line sweeps over it as the card opens.
  - **Card finder**: before the AI looks, OpenCV (a computer vision library, about 11 MB, downloaded once) finds the straight edges around your click and picks out the card (or its art box), even on a busy playmat with other cards touching it. The card is then straightened out, so turned, tapped, upside-down, and tilted cards are read squarely. If it can't find an outline, the matcher falls back to trying boxes of likely sizes around the click. The outline is dashed amber when it's only a closest match, and flashes red when nothing was found.
  - An AI model (DINOv2-small) runs in your browser, on your graphics card through WebGPU when available, and finds the **closest match** among the cards it knows. The first use downloads it (about 25 to 45 MB), and it is cached after that.
  - On the hosted page it can find **any Magic card**: a GitHub Action (`.github/workflows/card-index.yml` running `tools/build-index.mjs`) fingerprints every unique card artwork on Scryfall and publishes the index with the site. Each player downloads it once (about 20 MB, covering about 52,000 artworks). Commanders at the table get a small head start.
  - Without the index (a local file, or before the first build finishes) it knows the table's commanders and every card anyone looks up.
  - When you click another player's card, their browser sends a full-quality still of their camera, because the live video you receive is compressed.
  - When it isn't sure, it still shows its closest match with runners-up underneath. Picking the right one teaches it how that card looks on your camera, so it gets better as you play.
- **Matching log**: every card click is recorded in your browser (how sure the matcher was, and whether you corrected it). Open **Matching log** under the card search and click **Export log** to share it, so the matcher's "sure enough" settings can be tuned from real games.
- **Game records**: starting a new game asks who won, and the result (winner, commanders, length, turns, final life) is saved in every player's browser. The **Game records** panel shows wins per player and exports to CSV.
- **Card history**: under the card you're reading, a picture of every card you've looked up, been shown, or heard played, newest first (like SpellTable's), so you can jump back to any of them. Saved in your browser. Clicking a card name in the captions or the table log scrolls the panel up to the card.
- **Captions follow the game**: captions switch on when a player says what they're doing, and show just that: "I take 3 damage", "I gain 2 life", "I heal 4", "I pay 2 life", "I play Sol Ring", "I cast Counterspell and Swords to Plowshares". Card names are underlined (click one to read it); a card that can't be made out shows as "_". A card named without "I play" ("that Rhystic Study is brutal") is captioned and pops up too, at most once a minute. To keep everyday talk from popping up cards, each name gets a score from the kind of name (unusual words like Sol Ring score high; names made of everyday words like Rest in Peace or Balance score low), whether it's a commander at the table or a card seen recently, and how it was said (a Magic verb before it like "cast" or "tutor for", "my" or "your", a trigger or "is busted" after it, other Magic words in the sentence). So "rest in peace grandpa" pops up nothing, but "exile it with Rest in Peace" does. `tools/eval/mention-eval.mjs` measures this: on its held-out sentences, 14 of 15 card mentions found and 1 of 20 everyday sentences with a false popup (5 of 20 before). Other talk isn't captioned. Voice card lookups still hear everything. Turn it off under Captions to caption every word.
- **Terms library**: the **Terms** tab lists every Magic keyword, keyword action, and ability word (from Scryfall's lists, so new sets are included) plus game terms like the stack, exile, upkeep, and commander damage, with what each means and a link to its full rules. Search it by name or meaning.
- **Card names in captions**: names said the way people say them still link: "soul ring" finds Sol Ring, "commander sphere" Commander's Sphere, and a legend's short name ("Gwenom", even heard as "Gwen um") the full card.
- **Cards you say you play**: say "I play Sol Ring", "I'm casting Counterspell", or "I cast Rhystic Study and Arcane Signet" and the cards pop up on your camera for everyone, one after another (6 seconds each while more are waiting, 10 for the last; click one to read it), and the table log records them. Your browser works out the cards once and sends them with your caption, so every screen shows the same cards. Say "I play my commander" (or "I cast my commanders" for partners) and it shows the commander you set. Lands aren't shown. If a card name can't be made out, your screen says "Please repeat what card you played". Turn it off under Captions.
- **Captions and voice commands**: they use Chrome's or Edge's built-in speech recognition, with your deck's card names and the players' names as hints. In recent desktop Chrome you can pick **Chrome on-device** so audio never leaves your computer. The only voice commands are card lookups like "what does Rhystic Study do", "show me Sol Ring", or "look up Smothering Tithe". Nothing else changes by voice (life, turns, dice, counters, mana): use the buttons. "I take 3" and the like are only captioned.
  - Each player's own browser normally captions their own speech. When a friend's captions aren't coming through (turned off, a browser without speech to text, or a blocked speech service), your computer captions them from the audio you already hear, using Whisper (an open speech model, about 80 MB, downloaded once). It steps aside as soon as their own captions arrive. The Captions panel shows each player's status, with a checkbox to turn this off and a button to ask them to turn their captions on.
  - **Lisp-friendly matching**: tick **I have a lisp** on your own screen (this also helps your voice commands), or **Has a lisp** next to a player. "Th", "sh" and "z" then count as "s" when matching card names, so "Thol Ring" still links Sol Ring.

## Tests

**Matching benchmark**: **Actions > Matching benchmark** measures how often a click finds the right card. It draws real cards from Scryfall onto made-up webcam pictures (busy playmats, neighboring cards, turned and tilted cards, glare, blur, color cast, noise, compression) and runs the page's own matcher with the real AI model and index. The run's summary shows the share of right answers for the old click matching, the card finder, and a perfectly found card, and its files include pictures of the misses.

`tests/` holds browser tests that play the game with two or three Chromium players (joining, rejoining, life and turns, card matching, captions, decks, and the index builder). GitHub runs them on every pull request and every change to `main`. See `tests/README.md` to run them yourself.

## Troubleshooting

- **A friend sees the table but never gets video**: one of you is behind a strict network. Add a TURN relay (Metered, Twilio) under **Connection settings** in the lobby. Both players do this, then rejoin.
- **The AI matcher says it couldn't load**: the basic color-based picture matcher still works. The model downloads from huggingface.co and the library from cdn.jsdelivr.net, so check that neither is blocked.
- **Clicks only find commanders and cards someone looked up**: the all-cards index hasn't been built yet, or only partly. Run **Actions > Build card index**; its log says how many artworks are indexed and which model it used.
